We are based in Smithville, in DeKalb County, and most of our installed work sits within about an hour of it. That proximity is not a marketing line — it changes what we can promise about installation and about what happens on a Friday night in October when something stops working.

Where our boards are

Our installed and in-progress work across Middle Tennessee includes:

  • Murfreesboro — Riverdale High School baseball and softball, and Siegel High School baseball. Rutherford County is our densest cluster.
  • Carthage — Smith County High School, with an indoor gymnasium video board and a soccer field scoreboard in progress.

If you are in Rutherford, DeKalb, Smith, Wilson, Cannon, or Warren County and want to stand in front of a working board before you spend six figures, that is usually arrangeable on short notice. We would rather you see one in person than judge it from a photograph.

Why local matters after the install

Most videoboard purchases are evaluated on the install. The part that determines whether you are happy in year four is what happens when a panel fails during a playoff game.

A national manufacturer will ship you a replacement panel and a support ticket number. Being an hour away means we can be on site, and it means the person who answers the phone knows which board is yours and how it was wired — because they were there when it went up.

The counties we cover from Smithville

Middle Tennessee school districts are our core market: Rutherford, Wilson, DeKalb, Smith, Cannon, Warren, Putnam, and the surrounding counties, plus the Nashville metro and Knoxville within comfortable driving distance. We work statewide and install nationwide — our founder put up stadium boards for Coppell ISD, Keller ISD, and Cedar Hill ISD in Texas before launching LPCREATIVE — but Middle Tennessee is where we are, and it is where we can do things a distant supplier cannot.

Working with Tennessee school districts

Most of our customers are public school districts and booster clubs, which means quotes have to survive a board meeting. We itemize accordingly: display, structure, electrical, installation labor, and control software and training as separate lines, so a project can be presented, questioned, and adjusted without going back to square one.

Booster-funded projects — common for baseball, softball, and soccer — often need a number that a fundraising committee can work toward over a season. Tell us that up front and we will scope it in phases rather than handing you one large figure.

What it costs

Six packaged sizes cover most Middle Tennessee high school projects, from an entry-level board through a full stadium display, each listed with dimensions, square footage and a starting price on our Display Packages section. Use it to build a realistic budget before you call anyone — including us.
