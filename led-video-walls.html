An LED video wall is a different product from a scoreboard, even though the underlying panel technology is related. A scoreboard shows game state to a crowd a hundred yards away. A video wall shows content to people standing close enough to read it — which changes almost every specification decision.

Where video walls make sense

  • Churches and worship spaces — lyrics, sermon support, IMAG of the platform, and announcements, in a room where the nearest seat may be twenty feet from the screen.
  • School gyms and multipurpose rooms — a display that serves assemblies and events as well as games.
  • Corporate and retail spaces — lobbies, showrooms, and anywhere a projector is currently losing a fight with ambient light.
  • Event venues — stage backdrops and content walls that need to look right on camera as well as in the room.

Why LED instead of a projector

The honest answer is ambient light. A projector needs a dim room; the image washes out as soon as you turn the lights up or open a door. An LED wall generates its own light, so it holds contrast in a bright sanctuary or a lobby with a glass frontage, and it does not need a throw distance, a lamp replacement schedule, or a ceiling mount in the middle of the room.

The tradeoff is upfront cost. A projector is cheaper on day one. An LED wall wins over a decade on brightness, maintenance, and the fact that it works in the room you actually have rather than the room a projector needs.

Pitch is the whole conversation indoors

For an indoor wall, viewing distance drives pitch and pitch drives cost, more sharply than any other decision. A rough rule: the closest viewer should be no nearer than about a meter per millimeter of pitch. A 2.5mm wall reads cleanly from around eight feet; a 10mm outdoor-grade panel needs thirty feet before it stops looking like a grid.

This is why an outdoor scoreboard quote and an indoor video wall quote of the same physical size can differ substantially. Tell us where the front row is and we will tell you the pitch you need — and, just as often, the pitch you do not need to pay for.

Content is the part people underestimate

A video wall is only as good as what runs on it, and the honest failure mode is a beautiful display showing a static logo for three years because nobody owned the content. Before we quote, we want to know who will run it week to week and what they are comfortable with. Sometimes the right answer is a simpler display and a workflow the volunteer team will actually use.

Structure, power, and service access

Indoor walls mount to a wall or a freestanding frame, both of which need to carry real weight and stay flat — a wall that bows by a few millimeters across its face shows visible seams. Panels also need service access, whether that is clearance behind for rear service or front-serviceable panels where there is no room behind. Getting that wrong turns a twenty-minute panel swap into a scaffolding job.

Getting a number

Indoor walls vary too widely for a single per-square-foot figure to be meaningful — pitch swings the cost more than size does. Give us the room dimensions, the distance to the nearest seat, and what you intend to show, and we will come back with an itemized quote within one business day.
